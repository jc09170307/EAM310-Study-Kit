7 · Six Healing Sounds
SoundOrganEffect (1-liner)
Xū 嘘LiverRemoves heat, brightens eyes
Hē 呵HeartExpels heat, reduces fire
Hū 呼SpleenDrives out heat, strengthens SP
Sì 呬LungCalms qi, moistens lungs
Chuī 吹KidneyRemoves wind-cold, urges stagnant qi
Xī 嘻Triple BurnerClears heat/restlessness, emotions
Heads Up — Hū 呼 = both "Shout" (Wood voice sound) AND "Spleen" Healing Sound. Same hanzi, two different tables — don't conflate.

9 · The Five (Named) Odors
OdorDescriptor
Rancid (Sōu 餿)Fat no longer fresh, mown grass, unpleasant
ScorchedBurnt toast, dryer-warm clothes, scorched ironing
Fragrant (Xiāng 香)Cloying, sickly sweet — NOT actually pleasant
Rotten (Làn 爛)Rotting meat / garbage truck
Putrid (Fǔ 腐)Rotting meat / stagnant pond / stale urine
Heads Up — These 5 odor slides do NOT explicitly label a Zang-Fu element per odor (unlike Five Element Sounds). "Scorched" slide reuses xiào 笑 (same hanzi as Laughing) — likely a copy/paste error; classical scorched is jiāo 焦. Confirm with Prof. Schrier; don't assume the classical Wood/Fire/Earth/Metal/Water pairing without confirming in class.

Recognition Traps — Common Miscalls
TrapCorrect Discriminator
"Fragrant" odor = pleasantOn these slides, Fragrant = cloying/sickly-sweet, NOT a pleasant finding
Hū 呼 always means "Shout"Also the Spleen Healing Sound — check which table you're in
All 5 odors map cleanly to 5 ElementsSlides don't label element per odor; don't assume the classical pairing without confirming
Chronic hoarseness always = Blood stagnationChronic + worse in evening = Lung/Kidney Yin deficiency instead
Loud sound always = ExcessTrue for voice/cough/borborygmi/belching/yawning — but pair with onset & other signs before committing
Heads Up — Quiz Move: For every hearing/smelling finding: sound/odor quality → loud/foul vs. weak/faint → Full/Excess/Heat vs. Empty/Deficiency/Cold → organ → one confirming detail.
